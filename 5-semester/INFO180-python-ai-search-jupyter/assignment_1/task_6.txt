Comparison and conclusion:

Breadth-first search found the path with the fewest steps (length 115), 
but because it does not account for costs, the path cost was relatively high (351). 
It also visited many nodes (6452) and took almost five seconds to run.

Depth-first search was faster and visited fewer nodes (787),
but the solution was very poor: the path length was 737 and the cost 2129. 
This shows that DFS is not a reliable strategy for this problem.

Lowest-cost-first produced the optimal path cost of 255,
with a length of 130. However, it had to explore almost as many nodes as breadth-first (6450), 
and therefore took the longest time of all the admissible strategies (5.7 seconds).

The heuristic searches improved efficiency. Using MaxDimH still gave the optimal cost of 255,
but reduced the number of visited nodes to 5064 and time to 4.1 seconds. 
PythagorasH (Euclidean) performed even better, with only 4972 nodes visited and slightly faster time, 
while still giving the optimal cost of 255 and path length 130.

ApproxMaxDimH (3×MaxDimH) was by far the fastest and most efficient, 
solving the problem in 0.18 seconds with only 280 nodes visited. 
However, it did not guarantee optimality, producing a cost of 282 instead of 255.

In summary, breadth-first finds the shortest path in terms of steps but not cost, 
depth-first is inefficient and unreliable, lowest-cost-first guarantees the optimal cost but is slow, 
while MaxDimH and PythagorasH provides the best balance of optimality and efficiency. 

Among these, the Euclidean heuristic performs best. ApproxMaxDimH is the fastest but sacrifices optimality.
